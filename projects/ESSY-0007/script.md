# ESSY-0007

Episode title (visual-only, NOT narration — see `titles.json`):
So What?

Frozen approved English narration from Bilingual Narration Draft v6. The series
and episode titles are visual-only (`tts: false`, `role: title`) and must never
enter TTS input or narration arrays.

## N001

What does it mean to spend years building something?

What if the world barely notices?

And if none of it seems to matter, why do we keep going?

I have spent much of my working life in wireless communications.

Trying to make the systems more reliable, more efficient, and a little better with every improvement.

Most people shouldn't have to think about how their wireless connection works.

That's rather the point.

## N002

But every now and then, I look at what all this technology is being used for.

Someone is watching an endless series of AI-generated short dramas.

One episode after another.

And I find myself thinking:

Decades of research.

Extraordinary engineering.

Billions spent building networks.

And sometimes, I wonder if all that effort was really for something like this.

It's impressive.

And a little ridiculous.

Of course, I know the technology serves far more important purposes.

But still, I can't help feeling a little disappointed.

## N003

Sometimes I wonder what I actually contributed.

Algorithms I worked on.

Pieces of code I wrote.

Problems that took weeks or months to understand.

Perhaps somewhere, in some product, a small part of my work is still running.

Only at moments like that do I feel a small sense of having contributed something.

Not because it proves I was important.

But because something I once worked hard on might still be quietly doing its job.

And that gives me a small sense of reassurance.

## N004

But if I hadn't been there, someone else would probably have solved those problems.

Maybe sooner.

Maybe later.

Maybe with a better algorithm.

Or a worse one.

The products would probably still have been built.

And the world would look almost exactly the same.

I don't find that disappointing.

Most of us contribute small pieces to things much larger than ourselves.

And those pieces can usually be replaced.

Including mine.

## N005

The strange thing is, none of this made the work less interesting.

I still remember the satisfaction of finally understanding a problem that had resisted every attempt to solve it.

Some solutions were satisfying.

Others were less than perfect.

But finally understanding something that had puzzled me was always rewarding.

Not because I had changed the world.

But because something that hadn't made sense yesterday finally made sense today.

I enjoyed that.

I still do.

## N006

And looking back, I was fortunate.

Someone was willing to pay me to solve problems I was reasonably interested in, and capable of solving.

That was a pretty good arrangement.

Not every project was important.

Some work was tedious.

And some solutions probably mattered less than we thought.

But there were enough interesting questions to keep me going.

For quite a long time, as it turns out.

## N007

Now, as I begin to think about the years beyond work, I sometimes look back from a greater distance.

The projects.

The deadlines.

The technical arguments.

The little victories that seemed so important at the time.

Most have already faded.

Technology keeps changing.

New engineers arrive.

New problems appear.

Old solutions become obsolete.

And the work goes on.

Without needing me.

There's something comforting about that.

But also a little sadness.

## N008

Maybe that's simply how life goes.

We spend years doing things we take seriously.

And one day, we look back.

Not exactly with regret.

Just a slight sense of emptiness.

And perhaps a quiet sense of wondering what it was all for.
